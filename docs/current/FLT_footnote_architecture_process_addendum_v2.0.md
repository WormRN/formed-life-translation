# Formed Life Translation — Footnote Architecture Process Addendum v2.0

**Authority:** Governing process addendum subordinate to the FLT Constitution v2.0 and read with the Style Guide v2.0.  
**Adopted by:** Human Editor  
**Adoption date:** 2026-08-11

## Purpose

FLT protects a clear sixth-grade reading experience while preserving scholarly transparency and a durable internal record of editorial judgment. Notes therefore belong to three distinct layers. A concern must be routed to the correct layer rather than automatically cluttering the reading edition or halting production.

## Official tags

### `[rn]` — Reader Note

A Reader Note contains cultural, historical, conceptual, or major manuscript context that is materially useful for a religion-naive reader to understand the passage or a dynamic rendering.

- Visible in standard reading editions.
- Written in plain English for the FLT target reader.
- Used sparingly.
- Must not repair a reading text that is itself materially misleading or incomplete.
- Appropriate for an unfamiliar concept that the reading text renders dynamically but that ordinary readers still need help understanding.

### `[tn]` — Transparency Note

A Transparency Note documents source-language accountability without burdening the standard reading experience.

Typical content includes:

- literal Greek anchors;
- semantic range explanations;
- word-family or discourse links hidden by a dynamic rendering;
- minor manuscript variants;
- alternative syntactic attachments or viable contextual senses;
- explanation of why a dynamic reading remains source-traceable.

Publication behavior:

- Hidden in standard reading editions.
- Visible in Study and Scholarly editions.
- Written for interested non-specialists and reviewers; Greek may be identified when useful, but the note should explain its significance in ordinary English.

A `[tn]` concern is not, by itself, a reason to make the reading text more literal or to halt production when the semantic floor of the reading text remains intact.

### `[dl]` — Decision Log

A Decision Log entry records internal editorial governance and is never reader-facing.

Typical content includes:

- Human Editor overrides of auditor objections;
- rationale for choosing among viable interpretations;
- precedent-setting scope or attachment decisions;
- reasons an audit warning or block was accepted without changing the reading text;
- workflow history needed for later scholarly audit.

Publication behavior:

- Repository only.
- Invisible in standard, Study, and Scholarly reader editions.
- May quote auditor findings and preserve technical rationale more fully than reader-facing notes.

## Routing rule

Every note-worthy finding must be classified before it can affect passage state:

1. If the reading text materially loses, adds, or misleads on source meaning, repair the text. A note cannot cure a failed semantic floor.
2. If comprehension requires contextual help for the target reader, route to `[rn]`.
3. If the reading text is semantically defensible but source accountability would benefit from disclosure, route to `[tn]`.
4. If the matter concerns Human Editor judgment, precedent, or override rationale rather than reader information, route to `[dl]`.

A single issue may generate more than one tier only when the audiences genuinely need different information.

## Audit behavior

Auditors must distinguish a reading-text semantic block from a missing-note classification issue.

- Missing `[rn]` material may block acceptance only when the target reader would otherwise be materially misled or unable to recover necessary meaning.
- Missing `[tn]` material normally creates a Librarian backlog item, not a reading-text block, when the reading text independently passes the semantic floor.
- `[dl]` items never belong in a reader-note requirement and never block the reading edition merely because the internal rationale has not yet been published to readers.
- The Human Editor may override an auditor's note-layer classification. The override must be recorded as `[dl]`.

## Librarian backlog

The Librarian maintains a durable backlog of `[rn]` and `[tn]` obligations. Backlog status is independent of passage translation status unless the Human Editor explicitly makes a note a prerequisite for acceptance.

Each backlog item records at minimum:

- passage reference;
- tier tag;
- issue/topic;
- source or audit trigger;
- required edition visibility;
- status;
- Human Editor directives or precedent references.

## Drafting and candidate apparatus

Passage-Sense briefs must identify whether a note concern belongs to `[rn]`, `[tn]`, or `[dl]` when known. Candidate workers may propose reader-facing explanatory wording only for `[rn]` obligations. `[tn]` material belongs in the study apparatus/backlog and must not be forced into the sixth-grade reading text. `[dl]` material is withheld from blind workers.

## Governing principle

**The reading text carries the meaning. Reader Notes aid comprehension. Transparency Notes defend traceability. Decision Logs preserve governance. None of the three may be confused with the others.**
