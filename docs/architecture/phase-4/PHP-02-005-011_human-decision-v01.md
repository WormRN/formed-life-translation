## Approved benchmark text

**5** Have the same mindset toward one another that Christ Jesus had.

**6** Though He existed in the very nature of God, He did not treat His equality with God as something to use for His own advantage.

**7** Instead, He chose not to exercise His divine rights. He took on the true nature of a servant and was born as a human being.

**8** And as a man, He humbled Himself in obedience all the way to death—His death on a cross.

**9** Because of this, God elevated Him to the highest place of honor and gave Him the name above every other name,

**10** so that at the name of Jesus every knee will bow—in heaven, on the earth, and under the earth—

**11** and every tongue will openly declare that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

## Human decisions after audit

- Retain “chose not to exercise His divine rights.” The three-reader reconstruction and three-agent semantic-floor audit consistently understood this as voluntary nonuse of divine privilege without loss of deity.
- Revise verse 8 from “He lived in humble obedience” to “He humbled Himself in obedience,” restoring the explicit reflexive action while retaining natural English.
- Accept the compression of the doubled humanity language in verses 7–8. The audit found no material semantic loss.
- Preserve a transparent explanatory footnote for the consequential interpretive choices in verses 6–7. The apparatus should record meaningful alternatives and the rationale for the body wording.

## Footnote direction

Footnotes are not to be minimized merely for visual economy. FLT may use numerous notes when they help readers or future reviewers see:

- a meaningful translation alternative;
- an interpretive decision represented in the body text;
- the reason a traditional rendering was not used;
- a theological misunderstanding the chosen wording is designed to avoid; or
- a decision that may establish precedent elsewhere.

The notes should remain accessible to religion-naive readers. They need not reproduce the scale or technical depth of the NET Full Notes, but FLT values the same kind of transparency.

For this unit, the notes should explain that verse 6 represents the “use for His own advantage” understanding of the debated expression and that verse 7 describes voluntary nonexercise of divine rights, not Christ ceasing to be God.
