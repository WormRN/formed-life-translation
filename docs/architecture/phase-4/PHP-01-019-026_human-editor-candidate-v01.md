## Reading text

**19** I know that my distress will become my deliverance through your prayers and the help of the Spirit of Jesus Christ.

**20** My eager hope and expectation is that I will not be ashamed in any way. Instead, I hope to have complete courage so that now, as always, Christ will be honored in my body, whether I live or die.

**21** For to me, living means living for Christ, and dying means gaining something even better.

**22** But if living on in this body means more fruitful work for me, then I honestly don’t know which one to choose!

**23** I am torn between the two. I long to leave this life and be with Christ—that would be far better.

**24** But for your sake, it is more necessary that I remain in this body.

**25** Since I am convinced of this, I know I will stay. I will remain with all of you so that you can grow and experience joy in your faith.

**26** Then, when I return to you, you will have even more reason to be proud of what Christ Jesus has done in me.

## Human editorial decisions

- **1:19 — this / distress / deliverance:** “My distress” resolves the Greek’s open “this” as Paul’s adverse situation. “Deliverance” preserves rescue, vindication, and salvation resonance. The narrowing from “this” to “my distress” remains an explicit audit point.
- **1:20 — embodied honor:** Retains “in my body” and the live-or-die contrast while dividing the thought into natural English sentences.
- **1:21 — living and dying:** Explains “living is Christ” as “living for Christ.” “Something even better” interprets gain in light of the fuller comparison in verse 23; this anticipatory clarification remains an audit point.
- **1:22–24 — embodied life and departure:** Preserves fruitful work, Paul’s genuine dilemma, departure to be with Christ, and the necessity of remaining for the congregation.
- **1:25 — progress and joy:** Makes Paul’s expected continued presence serve both growth and joy in their faith.
- **1:26 — joyful boasting in Christ:** “Be proud of what Christ Jesus has done in me” interprets the difficult boast/pride construction and its relationship to Paul’s return. The Greek more directly locates their increased boasting in Christ in relation to Paul; this wording remains a substantive audit point.
