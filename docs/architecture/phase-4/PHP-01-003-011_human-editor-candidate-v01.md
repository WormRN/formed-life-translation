## Reading text

**3** Every time I think of you, I thank my God.

**4** Whenever I pray for all of you, I always pray with joy.

**5** I am so grateful for how you have partnered with me in spreading the good news, from the very first day until now.

**6** I am certain that God, who began this good work in you, will continue it until it is finished on the day Christ Jesus returns.

**7** Because you are so dear to me, it is right for me to feel this way about all of you. We share together in God’s grace, whether I am in prison or defending the good news and showing that it is true.

**8** God is my witness how deeply I long for all of you with the tender affection of Christ Jesus.

**9** I pray that your love will grow more and more, guided by true knowledge and good judgment,

**10** so that you can recognize and choose what matters most. Then you will be pure and blameless when Christ returns.

**11** Your lives will be filled with the good fruit of right living that comes through Jesus Christ, bringing glory and praise to God.

## Human editorial decisions

- **1:3–5 — remembrance, joy, and partnership:** Uses warm first-person language and retains the congregation’s sustained partnership in spreading the good news.
- **1:6 — work in you:** Retains the plural “in you,” allowing both personal and congregational resonance, while expressing completion at Christ’s return naturally.
- **1:7 — heart and shared grace:** “Because you are so dear to me” recreates the relational force of holding them in Paul’s heart. “We share together” makes their joint participation in grace explicit.
- **1:7 — defense and confirmation:** “Defending the good news and showing that it is true” unpacks the two coordinated ideas for a religion-naive reader.
- **1:8 — affection of Christ:** Retains the Christ-related source or quality of Paul’s tender affection without resolving the phrase more narrowly.
- **1:9 — love with knowledge and discernment:** “Guided by” interprets knowledge and good judgment as governing the growth of love. This is a deliberate dynamic relationship and should remain visible in the audit record.
- **1:10 — discernment and outcome:** “Recognize and choose what matters most” expresses both evaluation and response. The result is stated in short, orally manageable sentences.
- **1:11 — fruit of righteousness:** “Good fruit of right living” preserves the metaphor while making its ethical effect accessible. Its source through Jesus Christ and purpose in God’s glory remain explicit.
