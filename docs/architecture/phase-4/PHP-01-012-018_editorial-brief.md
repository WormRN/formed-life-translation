## Consolidated human decisions

The generated ensemble brief contained 23 entries, many of which repeated the same questions. They reduce to five substantive decisions:

1. **Verse 13 — *praetorium*.** Adopt the deliberately broad “entire guard,” or choose the more concrete “imperial guard” or “palace guard.” Recommendation: **entire guard** in the text, with the fuller historical alternatives retained for a note.
2. **Verse 13 — *en Christō*.** Use “in chains for Christ,” “imprisoned because of Christ,” or broader “imprisonment is connected with Christ.” Recommendation: **in chains for Christ** as the clearest balance of cause, allegiance, and natural English.
3. **Verse 17 — distress and chains.** Prefer structurally close “add distress to my chains” or natural “add to my suffering while I am in prison.” Recommendation: **the natural rendering**, because the structural version gives chains an unnatural role in English.
4. **Verse 18 — pretense/truth.** Preserve the broader contrast or specify false/true motives. Recommendation: **pretense … truth**, allowing context to communicate motive without narrowing the lexical range.
5. **Verse 18 — future joy.** Use simple “will rejoice” or infer continuity with “will continue to rejoice.” Recommendation: **will rejoice**, preserving the present–future repetition without adding aspect.

## Decisions already supported strongly enough for the proposed text

- Put “brothers and sisters” first in verse 12.

- Attach “in the Lord” to confidence in verse 14.
- Leave “the message” unexpanded in verse 14.
- Preserve purposeful placement in verse 16 without naming the agent.
- Use dignified repeated “rejoice” in verse 18.
