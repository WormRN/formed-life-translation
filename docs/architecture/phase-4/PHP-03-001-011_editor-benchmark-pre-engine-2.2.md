## Editor draft preserved as submitted

1 And now, my brothers and sisters, rejoice because you belong to the Lord. I do not mind writing these same things to you again and it is for your protection.

2 Watch out for those dogs! Watch out for those who do evil! Watch out for those who insist on cutting the body.

3 We are the people who bear the sign of belonging to God.* (footnote to explain something like: literally “we are the circumcision.” Circumcision being the sign that a person was one of God’s chosen people.) We worship by the Spirit of God and find our worth in Christ Jesus. And we put no confidence in human credentials.

4 Though if anyone could have confidence in such credentials, it would be me. If someone else thinks he has reason to trust in them, I have even more.

5 I was circumcised when I was eight days old. I am an Israelite by birth and a member of the the tribe of Benjamin - a true Hebrew born of Hebrew parents. In my commitment to the law, I was a Pharisee.

6 I was so passionate that I fiercely persecuted the church. As for righteousness measure by keeping the law, no one could find fault with me.

7 But whatever I once considered as profit, I now count as a loss because of Christ.

8 Yes, I consider everything a complete loss compared to the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord. I have given it all up for Him considering it all waste, so that I can gain Christ

9 and belong completely to Him. I do not claim a right standing with God that I crafted by obeying the Law. Instead, I have the standing that comes from God, based on my reliance on Christ - a status received by faith.

10 My goal is to know Christ intimately, to experience the power that raised Him from the dead, and to share in His sufferings, becoming just like Him in His death,

11 so that, somehow, I may also be raised from the dead.

## Previously agreed contextual clarification for verse 3

The editor subsequently agreed that verse 3 is best analyzed as a corporate identity claim: Paul applies the identity of God’s authentic covenant people to those who worship by God’s Spirit, center their confidence in Christ, and reject human credentials. The agreed reader-note direction was:

> Literally, “we are the circumcision.” Circumcision was the physical sign identifying Israel as God’s covenant people. Here Paul applies that identity to those who worship by God’s Spirit, find their confidence in Christ, and do not rely on human ancestry or achievement.

This clarification informs the source-side semantic brief; the benchmark wording above remains unchanged.
