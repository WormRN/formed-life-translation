## Reading text

**12** I want you to know, brothers and sisters, that what has happened to me has actually helped spread the good news.

**13** Everyone in the imperial guard, and everyone else, now knows that I am in prison because of Christ.

**14** And my imprisonment has given most of the brothers and sisters new confidence in the Lord, so that they now dare to speak God’s message even more boldly, without fear.

**15** Some preach about Christ because they are jealous and want to compete. Others preach with good intentions.

**16** Those who preach out of love know I have been given the task of defending the good news.

**17** The others preach about Christ only to advance themselves. Their motives are not sincere, and they think they can make my suffering in prison worse.

**18** But what does that matter? The important thing is that Christ is being preached, whether the motives are false or sincere. And in that I take great joy. Yes, I will continue to be full of joy.

## Deliberate decisions

- **1:12 — address placement:** The address remains inside the sentence because it performed best in the human editor’s audio review.
- **1:13 — imperial guard:** The reading text chooses the concrete guard interpretation. The broader lexical/historical alternatives remain appropriate for the study apparatus.
- **1:14 — God’s message:** The possessive is supplied contextually for reader clarity. Greek has the more open expression “the message/word”; this dynamic clarification should remain documented.
- **1:15 — want to compete / good intentions:** The rendering makes the contrasting motives immediately understandable without specifying Paul as the target of the competition.
- **1:16 — given the task:** The wording retains purposeful placement without naming the assigning agent.
- **1:17 — advance themselves:** This renders the selfish purpose in direct contemporary English. The second sentence makes Paul the sufferer and imprisonment the setting of the intended harm.
- **1:18 — joy language:** “Take great joy” and “continue to be full of joy” recreate the rhetorical force and present-to-future movement dynamically. “Great,” “full,” and explicit continuity are interpretive expansions rather than direct lexical equivalents and remain marked for final human review.
