## Human review notes

### Philippians 1:17

Current wording:

> They think they can make my suffering in prison worse.

This is a defensible dynamic resolution. It makes Paul the sufferer and retains the connection to imprisonment. The Greek leaves open whether the added distress is emotional, reputational, legal, or practical. The current English does not specify which kind, though it does frame all of it as suffering in prison. No repair is required unless the human editor prefers to preserve greater openness.

### Philippians 1:18

Current wording:

> And in that I take great joy. Yes, I will continue to be full of joy.

The Greek explicitly presents current and future rejoicing. The repeated clauses provide rhetorical support for an expressive dynamic rendering, but “great” and “full” add a modest degree of intensity, while “continue” makes ongoing continuity explicit. One auditor warned; two accepted the move. This remains a human decision, not a fidelity failure.
