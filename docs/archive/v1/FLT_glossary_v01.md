# FLT — Glossary / Key-Term Policy (v01 Starter)

**Project:** Formed Life Translation (FLT)  
**Document type:** Glossary — recurring Greek terms and translation decisions  
**Status:** v01 starter  
**Created:** 2026-06-12  

> This starter glossary is not exhaustive. It exists to prevent repeated re-litigation of common terms while still allowing context-sensitive translation. Each entry should grow with examples, exceptions, and decision-log links.

---

## 1. Glossary Tiers

### Tier 1 — Keep

Terms clear enough, common enough, or important enough to retain in the body text.

### Tier 2 — Context-Weighed

Terms with several possible renderings. The context decides. The glossary gives the menu and decision rule.

### Tier 3 — Unpack

Terms likely to be unknown or misleading to the primary audience. These normally receive an unpacked rendering in the body text.

## 3. εὐαγγέλιον

**Lemma:** εὐαγγέλιον  
**Strong’s:** G2098  
**Tier:** 3 — Unpack  
**Default rendering:** good news  
**Possible renderings:** good news; message of good news; announcement; good news about Christ  
**Constitutional invariant:** Must always carry the sense of an announcement of something God has done.  

**Decision rule:** Use “good news” as the default. Add “about Christ,” “of Christ,” or “from God” when context requires. Avoid using “gospel” in the body text unless a later style decision allows it in learned contexts.

**Footnote trigger:** First occurrence in a book may explain that “good news” translates the word often rendered “gospel.”

**Examples:** Phil 1:5, 1:7, 1:12, 1:16, 1:27.

---

## 4. Χριστός

**Lemma:** Χριστός  
**Strong’s:** G5547  
**Tier:** 1 with first-use explanation  
**Default rendering:** Christ  
**Possible renderings:** Christ; Messiah; Anointed One  

**Decision rule:** Use “Christ” in the body text for consistency. Explain at first occurrence in each book that Christ means Messiah / Anointed One.

**Footnote trigger:** First occurrence in each book.

**Suggested note:** Christ means “Anointed One,” the promised Messiah.

---

## 5. κύριος

**Lemma:** κύριος  
**Strong’s:** G2962  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** Lord; lord; master; sir; owner  

**Decision rule:**

- Referring to God or Christ → Lord
- Human superior / owner → master or lord, depending context
- Polite address → sir
- OT quotation where Hebrew source has YHWH → encode `{{divine-name:Lord}}` and footnote the Hebrew divine-name background

**Footnote trigger:** Divine-name quotation instances; contested referent; major interpretive choice.

---

## 6. δοῦλος

**Lemma:** δοῦλος  
**Strong’s:** G1401  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** servant; slave; bondservant; one who belongs to; household servant  

**Decision rule:** Use “servant” when the context emphasizes devoted service and “slave” would create an English distraction greater than the Greek point. Use “slave” or a stronger phrase when ownership, social status, or master/slave contrast is central.

**Footnote trigger:** Where “servant” may soften the force of δοῦλος.

**Example:** Phil 1:1 currently “servants of Christ Jesus.”

---

## 7. ἅγιος / ἅγιοι

**Lemma:** ἅγιος  
**Strong’s:** G40  
**Tier:** 3 — Unpack for people  
**Default rendering for believers:** God’s holy people  
**Possible renderings:** holy; holy people; God’s holy people; saints  

**Decision rule:** For believers, prefer “God’s holy people” because “saints” is misunderstood by religion-naive readers. Use “holy” for things, places, Spirit, or moral quality where clear.

**Footnote trigger:** First occurrence in a book or where traditional “saints” may be expected.

**Example:** Phil 1:1.

---

## 8. χάρις

**Lemma:** χάρις  
**Strong’s:** G5485  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** grace; favor; undeserved favor; gracious gift; kindness  

**Decision rule:** Retain “grace” in fixed greetings, dense theological contexts, and where the term itself should be learned. Use “undeserved favor,” “gracious gift,” or similar where the context stresses the gift/favor sense and “grace” may be vague.

**Footnote trigger:** When rendering something other than “grace,” note the traditional rendering.

**Open question from Philippians 1:** Whether Phil 1:2 and 1:7 should read consistently as “grace” or vary as “grace / undeserved favor.”

---

## 9. πίστις / πιστεύω

**Lemmas:** πίστις; πιστεύω  
**Strong’s:** G4102; G4100  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** faith; trust; faithfulness; belief; confidence; the faith  

**Decision rule:**

- Personal reliance on Christ → trust / trust in
- The body of Christian belief → the faith
- The virtue or posture of reliance → faith
- Faithfulness sense → faithfulness, if context supports

**Footnote trigger:** Where “faith” vs “faithfulness” is debated, or where “trust” replaces traditional “believe.”

**Example:** Phil 1:29 uses “trust in Christ.”

---

## 10. δικαιοσύνη / δίκαιος

**Lemmas:** δικαιοσύνη; δίκαιος  
**Strong’s:** G1343; G1342  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** righteousness; right standing; justice; right living; right with God; faithful covenant justice  

**Decision rule:** Determine whether the context emphasizes status before God, ethical conduct, justice, covenant faithfulness, or visible fruit. Do not automatically use one English word.

**Footnote trigger:** Any major departure from “righteousness.”

**Example:** Phil 1:11 currently “good fruit that comes through Jesus Christ,” with righteousness understood as fruit-bearing conduct.

---

## 11. σάρξ

**Lemma:** σάρξ  
**Strong’s:** G4561  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** flesh; body; human nature; sinful nature; merely human; human weakness  

**Decision rule:** Never render automatically. Ask whether the context refers to physical body, human descent, mortal life, unaided human capacity, sinful orientation, or ordinary human existence.

**Footnote trigger:** Where traditional “flesh” is replaced and the choice affects interpretation.

**Examples:** Phil 1:22, 1:24 use “this body / stay alive” sense.

---

## 12. πνεῦμα

**Lemma:** πνεῦμα  
**Strong’s:** G4151  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** Spirit; spirit; breath; wind; inner life; attitude  

**Decision rule:** Use “Spirit” for the Holy Spirit. Use “spirit” for human spirit, disposition, shared attitude, or other non-divine senses. If contested, note the alternative.

**Open question from Philippians 1:** Whether Phil 1:27 should stay lowercase “one spirit” or carry a footnote acknowledging possible Holy Spirit reference.

---

## 13. σωτηρία / σῴζω

**Lemmas:** σωτηρία; σῴζω  
**Strong’s:** G4991; G4982  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** salvation; deliverance; rescue; saved; healed; made whole  

**Decision rule:** Use “salvation/saved” where ultimate spiritual rescue is clearly in view. Use “deliverance/rescue” where the context is broader, ambiguous, physical, legal, or situational.

**Footnote trigger:** Where “deliverance” replaces expected “salvation,” or where ambiguity is important.

**Example:** Phil 1:19 and 1:28 currently use deliverance/saved according to context and consistency.

---

## 14. ἀδελφοί

**Lemma:** ἀδελφός / ἀδελφοί  
**Strong’s:** G80  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** brothers; brothers and sisters; believers; fellow believers; family  

**Decision rule:** When addressing or referring to the whole church, use “brothers and sisters.” When the context refers generally to Christians but not in direct address, “believers” may be clearer.

**Footnote trigger:** Not normally needed after the policy is established.

**Examples:** Phil 1:12 “Brothers and sisters”; Phil 1:14 “believers.”

---

## 15. μετάνοια / μετανοέω

**Lemmas:** μετάνοια; μετανοέω  
**Strong’s:** G3341; G3340  
**Tier:** 2 or 3 — Context-weighed / often unpack  
**Possible renderings:** repent; turn back to God; change your heart and life; change your mind; turn around  

**Decision rule:** Because “repent” may be churchy but still important, choose per context. In proclamation contexts, “turn back to God” often communicates the force clearly. A first-use note may connect this to the traditional word “repent.”

**Footnote trigger:** First major occurrence in each Gospel/Acts.

---

## 16. νόμος

**Lemma:** νόμος  
**Strong’s:** G3551  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** law; Law; law of Moses; Scripture; principle; command  

**Decision rule:** Capitalize “Law” when referring to the Torah or Mosaic law as a recognized body. Use lowercase “law” for general principle or rule. Use “law of Moses” when clarity requires.

**Footnote trigger:** Where “Law” may be ambiguous or where Paul’s argument depends on the sense.

---

## 17. κόσμος

**Lemma:** κόσμος  
**Strong’s:** G2889  
**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed  
**Possible renderings:** world; people of the world; human system; creation; universe; worldly order  

**Decision rule:** Especially in John, ask whether κόσμος means created world, humanity, the world system opposed to God, or the realm of unbelief.

**Footnote trigger:** Where “world” is unpacked.

---

## 18. ἱλαστήριον / ἱλασμός Word Group

**Tier:** 2 — Context-weighed, with body-text restriction  
**Constitutional invariant:** “Propitiation” is forbidden in the body text.  
**Possible renderings:** sacrifice that deals with sin; atoning sacrifice; place of mercy; mercy seat; sacrifice of atonement  

**Decision rule:** Render according to context and textual image. Avoid technical terms that the target reader will not understand without explanation.

**Footnote trigger:** Always, where the term appears.

---

## 19. σπλάγχνα

**Lemma:** σπλάγχνον  
**Strong’s:** G4698  
**Tier:** 3 — Unpack  
**Possible renderings:** deep affection; compassion; tender mercy; heartfelt concern  

**Decision rule:** Do not translate literally as “bowels” in the body text. Use the emotional sense.

**Example:** Phil 1:8 “deep affection.”
