# Philippians 1 v0.2 — Validation Report

The promised checks had **not** been run when the previous status answer implied they were underway. This report records the checks now actually completed.

## 1. Assembly check

**Result: PASS**

The human-adjudicated wording has been assembled into a complete verse-numbered chapter. The verse 18–19 boundary has been corrected:

- verse 18 contains both present and future rejoicing;
- verse 19 begins with “I know that…”

## 2. Changed-lines-only Greek regression

### Verse 1

**Wording:** “To God’s holy people in Philippi, all of you who belong to Christ Jesus, together with the leaders and assistants.”

**Result:** PASS_WITH_NOTE

- πᾶσιν is represented by “all of you.”
- ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ is interpreted relationally as belonging to Christ.
- σύν is naturally represented by “together with.”
- “leaders and assistants” remains a scoped FLT decision.

### Verse 6

**Wording:** “He who began a good work among you will continue working until it is finished…”

**Result:** PASS_WITH_NOTE

- Beginning and completion are preserved.
- “Continue working” makes the ongoing process explicit.
- “He” has a clear divine referent from the preceding context.
- “On the day Christ Jesus returns” remains a contextual clarification of “the day of Christ Jesus.”

### Verse 7a

**Wording:** “I have good reason to feel this way…”

**Result:** PASS_WITH_NOTE

The Greek says it is “right” for Paul to think this way. “Good reason” shifts slightly from moral appropriateness toward justified confidence, but the contextual force remains intact.

### Verse 7b

**Wording:** “We share together in God’s grace, whether I am in prison or defending and confirming the truth of the good news.”

**Result:** PASS_WITH_NOTE

- Shared participation in χάρις is preserved.
- “God’s grace” is less contextually narrow than “privilege.”
- “Whether” treats both settings as circumstances across which the shared grace remains true.
- “The truth of” is an interpretive clarification of confirming the good news.

### Verse 10

**Wording:** “recognize what matters most”

**Result:** PASS

This communicates testing and discerning the things that truly matter or excel.

### Verses 12–14

**Result:** PASS

- Verse 12 preserves the reversal: imprisonment advances rather than hinders the good news.
- Verse 13 preserves the imperial-guard setting and public connection with Christ.
- Verse 14 preserves increased confidence, daring, speech, and freedom from fear.
- “More daring to speak” is slightly less idiomatic than “speak more boldly,” but more visibly retains the courage-to-act force of τολμᾶν.

### Verse 18

**Result:** PASS

The exclamation point is a defensible English representation of the emphatic shift from present rejoicing to continued future rejoicing.

### Verse 19

**Wording:** “my distress will become my deliverance”

**Result:** PASS_WITH_NOTE

The Greek says “this,” referring back to Paul’s present imprisonment, opposition, and affliction. “My distress” makes that implied referent explicit. It is a dynamic clarification rather than a literal gloss.

### Verse 27

**Result:** PASS_WITH_NOTE

- “Govern yourselves” preserves part of the public/civic force.
- “Reflects the true value” communicates conduct fitting the good news without implying merit.
- “United in one spirit” combines the unity language naturally.
- “Competing together as one team” preserves the athletic/team image.
- “Stay faithful to the good news” is a contextual resolution of the difficult faith/genitive phrase.

### Verse 29

**Result:** PASS

- No explicit “God” has been inserted.
- The passive “you have been given” is preserved.
- “For Christ’s sake” represents ὑπέρ.
- Trusting and suffering remain parallel gifts.
- “Gift” communicates the grace-root of ἐχαρίσθη.

## 3. Literary and oral regression

**Overall result: PASS_WITH_POLISH_NOTES**

The chapter still reads as one coherent letter. The emotional movement remains clear:

1. warm greeting;
2. joyful thanksgiving;
3. confidence in God’s work;
4. imprisonment advancing the good news;
5. Christ honored in life or death;
6. united courage under opposition.

### Strong transitions

- Verses 18–19 now work especially well:
  “That is why I rejoice and continue to rejoice! I know that my distress will become my deliverance…”

### Oral-flow notes for later—not mandatory changes

- Verse 7 is dense but understandable.
- Verse 14’s “more daring to speak” is faithful but slightly less natural aloud.
- Verse 27 is long and may benefit from breath-based paragraphing, though its present wording is coherent.
- Verse 29 is clear but intentionally weighty.

No literary issue currently requires reopening the Greek.
